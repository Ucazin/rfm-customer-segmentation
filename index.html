Project 06 · Marketing analytics · Customer segmentation

49.5% of revenue rides on 609 customers. Now what?

Two years of e-commerce data from a UK online retailer — 805,549 transactions, 5,878 customers, £17.7M of revenue. I segmented them, priced the next quarter's promotional plan, and projected a 10.5× ROI.

The story

The revenue is extraordinarily top-heavy. Champions — 609 customers averaging 8 days since their last order — account for £8.8M, or 49.5% of the book. Add Loyal and you're at 66% from 11% of the customers. Below that line, every additional segment matters less than keeping one Champion happy.

This is not a "long tail" story. It is "the long tail is the floor; the head is the building."

Where the at-risk dollars actually live

  • At Risk (177 customers, £845k) — used to be Loyal, just stopped buying. Recency between 60 and 290 days.
  • Can't Lose Them (11 customers, £98k) — used to be Champions. Now silent. Highest unit value in the book.
  • Combined: 188 customers, £943k of historical revenue, not currently buying.
Why not Lost? Lost is bigger on paper (1,892 customers, £3M of historical revenue), but Lost is by definition outside the window where a re-engagement campaign converts above cost. At Risk + Can't Lose Them are the people who were buying and just stopped — the right moment to intervene.

What the playbook recommends

  • VIP — Champions, Can't Lose Them. $6-22 per customer. High-touch human outreach. ROI of the Champions program alone: 23.8×.
  • Retain — Loyal, Potential Loyalist, At Risk. $2.50-18 per customer. Mid-touch cross-sell or targeted win-back.
  • Reactivate — Hibernating, About to Sleep, Need Attention. $0.80-5 per customer. Low-cost email. If it doesn't work, walk away.
  • Don't spend — Lost, Promising. A coupon to someone who hasn't ordered in a year is theatre.

Total quarterly cost: $13.7k. Projected retained / new revenue: $144.7k. Blended ROI: 10.5×.

What I'm honest about

  • Conversion rates are quoted from industry benchmarks (Klaviyo, Mailchimp, Optimove), not measured from your data. Re-run after one quarter of A/B and update.
  • RFM is a snapshot, not a trend. Production deployment re-runs this monthly.
  • The k-means alternative produced the same Champions cluster as the rule-based segmentation. That's the right outcome — it means the rule-based labels (which marketing can read) are not arbitrary.
